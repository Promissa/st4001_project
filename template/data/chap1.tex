\chapter{Introduction}

\section{Motivation}
Federated learning (FL) trains a shared model across distributed
clients without requiring those clients to upload raw data, which is
attractive for edge intelligence because it reduces privacy exposure
and keeps data near its source device~\cite{mcmahan2017communication}.
The communication cost of conventional digital FL grows with the number
of participating clients, because every client must send an update in a
separate resource block. Analog over-the-air aggregation (OTA-FL) changes
this by letting clients transmit updates as analog waveforms at the
same time, so that the wireless multiple-access channel physically
superposes the signals and the server observes a single aggregate in
one shared channel use.

OTA-FL therefore scales in clients, but the same superposition also
injects noise, interference, and possibly fading into the aggregate
update. When the interference is heavy-tailed, a rare impulsive sample
in a single round can be orders of magnitude larger than a typical
client pseudo-gradient, which makes plain FedAvg-style updates
oscillate or diverge. The reference work ``Adaptive Federated Learning
Over the Air''~\cite{wang2024adaptive} studies this regime under
fading and symmetric $\alpha$-stable interference. This project
follows that direction and makes the $\alpha$-stable tail index a
core experimental variable; AWGN is treated as the special case
$\alpha=2$.

\section{Problem Statement}
This thesis studies FL over a simulated analog OTA aggregation
channel. At communication round $t$, the server receives a noisy
aggregate update $\tilde{g}^t$:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{g}^t =
    \frac{1}{N\eta_{\rm loc}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N} \Delta_n^t + \xi^t\right),
\end{equation}
where $N$ is the number of clients, $\Delta_n^t$ is the pseudo-gradient
produced by client $n$, $\eta_{\rm loc}$ is the local SGD learning rate,
and $\xi^t$ is symmetric $\alpha$-stable channel noise. Smaller $\alpha$
produces heavier tails and more frequent impulsive outliers; $\alpha=2$
recovers the Gaussian AWGN case.

The central research question is concrete and experimentally testable:
\emph{how does the final test accuracy of FedAvg-OTA,
AdaGrad-OTA, and Adam-OTA depend on the tail index $\alpha$ and the
noise scale $\gamma$, when all three optimizers consume the same OTA
aggregate $\tilde{g}^t$?} The current codebase implements a controlled simulator for the core
heavy-tail mechanism ($\alpha$-stable OTA aggregation, adaptive server
updates, and MAC robust pre-processing) and evaluates a narrower
robustness claim.

\section{Contributions}
The contributions of this thesis are split into empirical findings and
reusable artifacts.

\noindent\textbf{Findings}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Under the implemented heavy-tail CIFAR-10 setting
    (ResNet-18, $N=100$, 100 rounds, $\gamma=0.05$, three seeds),
    AdaGrad-OTA and Adam-OTA complete all three seeds for every tested
    heavy-tail value, while FedAvg-OTA completes no runs
    for $\alpha\leq1.9$.
    This directly supports the hypothesis that fractional second-moment
    adaptation stabilizes training in the presence of $\alpha$-stable
    impulsive noise.
    \item The observed $\alpha$-trend for the adaptive methods is
    broadly monotone: final accuracy improves as $\alpha$ approaches the
    Gaussian endpoint. This agrees with the reference paper's qualitative
    claim that heavier tails slow training, although the absolute ranking
    between AdaGrad-OTA and Adam-OTA remains hyperparameter-dependent.
    \item A targeted learning-rate sweep at $\alpha=1.3$ shows that
    Adam-OTA's lower default result is partly an lr-mismatch effect:
    reducing $\eta$ from $10^{-2}$ to $10^{-3}$ raises its mean final
    accuracy from 26.70\% to 33.65\%. AdaGrad-OTA is best at the default
    $\eta=10^{-2}$ with 34.62\%, so tuning narrows but does not reverse
    the ranking in the tested grid.
    \item At $\alpha=1.3$, $\gamma=0.05$, $k=3$, Median Anchored
    Clipping stabilizes the fixed-step FedAvg-OTA baseline, changing it
    from chance-level 10.00\% with 0/3 successful no-MAC runs to
    20.34\% mean final accuracy with MAC, and improves the two adaptive
    methods by 2.73 and 4.97 percentage points.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Artifacts}
\begin{itemize}
    \item A modular PyTorch OTA-FL simulator with a synchronous
    FL training loop, four server optimizers that consume the same
    OTA aggregate, a symmetric $\alpha$-stable channel oracle based on
    the Chambers--Mallows--Stuck sampler, MAC pre-processing, JSON
    result export, and plotting scripts.
    \item A finite-trace stability analysis that explains why a
    fractional second-moment accumulator contracts the effective
    coordinate-wise learning rate on impulsive rounds, together with a
    tail-index scaling argument for long-run step-size decay.
\end{itemize}
