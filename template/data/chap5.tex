\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}

\section{Conclusion}
\ \ \ \ \, This thesis asks whether server-side adaptive optimization can act
as an implicit, CSI-free filter for an $\alpha$-stable OTA-FL channel.
The main supported claim is empirical: under the three-seed CIFAR-10
tail-index ablation with $\gamma=0.05$ and $\alpha\in\{1.1,1.3,1.5,
1.7,1.9\}$, AdaGrad-OTA and Adam-OTA complete all runs, while
FedAvg-OTA completes no runs for $\alpha\leq1.9$. AdaGrad-OTA reaches
22.12--35.35\% final accuracy across the heavy-tail sweep, and
Adam-OTA reaches 28.64--39.65\%. This supports the hypothesis that a
fractional second-moment accumulator contracts the effective server
learning rate on impulsive rounds and improves stability.

The claim is scoped rather than absolute. The observed $\alpha$-trend
for adaptive methods is broadly monotone and agrees with the qualitative
message that heavier tails slow training. In the updated MAC comparison at
$\alpha=1.3$, $\gamma=0.05$, $k=3$, MAC turns the FedAvg-OTA baseline from
chance-level 10.00\% with 0/3 successful no-MAC runs into 20.34\% mean final
accuracy and raises the adaptive-method final accuracies as well. The targeted
$\alpha=1.3$ learning-rate sweep shows that Adam-OTA improves from 26.70\% to
33.65\% when $\eta$ is reduced from
$10^{-2}$ to $10^{-3}$, but still does not exceed AdaGrad-OTA's best
34.62\%, which shows that fractional second-moment
normalization is not costless in the Gaussian regime.
The main output is therefore a reproducible PyTorch simulator with
$\alpha$-stable tail-index experiments, MAC robustness comparisons,
and a finite-trace stability analysis for the shrinkage mechanism,
rather than a complete non-convex convergence proof or line-by-line
reproduction of the reference paper.

\section{Limitations}
\ \ \ \ \, The project remains narrower than the full reference paper in four
ways. First, it provides simulator-aligned finite-trace stability
propositions rather than the full multi-dimensional non-convex
convergence proof in the reference paper. Second, the wireless
channel is a simulation oracle and does not model mobility, multi-cell
interference, synchronization errors, CSI acquisition, or power
control. Third, the project does not include a software-defined-radio
or real wireless testbed validation. Fourth, the empirical evidence is
bounded by three seeds and a targeted learning-rate sweep at only one
heavy-tailed operating point, so full optimizer rankings remain
tuning-dependent (see~\S\ref{sec:lr-sweep} and~\S\ref{sec:threats-validity}).

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future-work}
\ \ \ \ \, The most direct next step is to expand the targeted
learning-rate sweep into a full per-method $\eta$ and $\beta_2$ sweep for
each $\alpha$, including the FedAvg-style baselines. A second extension is
to replace the current additive-noise oracle with a richer wireless model
that includes fading, power control, and synchronization error. Finally, the
MAC sweep should be expanded across smaller $\alpha$, larger $\gamma$, and
several clipping factors $k$ to determine when the observed pre-processing
gain persists and when clipping begins to remove useful gradient signal.
