\centerline{\xiaoyi\bfseries Abstract}
\vspace{4em}

Over-the-air federated learning (OTA-FL) scales with the number of
clients by letting analog client updates superpose directly in the
wireless multiple-access channel, but the same superposition injects
channel noise into the server-side aggregate. When the interference is
heavy-tailed, rare but very large impulsive samples can dominate a
FedAvg-style update and destabilize training. This thesis studies whether
server-side adaptive optimization can improve robustness by responding to
noisy OTA aggregates: a fractional second-moment accumulator increases
normalization on impulsive coordinates and thereby reduces their effective
update scale without requiring channel state information (CSI).

We implement a PyTorch OTA-FL simulator with a symmetric
$\alpha$-stable channel oracle based on the Chambers--Mallows--Stuck
sampler, four server optimizers
(FedAvg-OTA, FedAvgM-OTA, AdaGrad-OTA, Adam-OTA) that all consume the
same noisy aggregate, Median Anchored Clipping (MAC) as a robust
pre-processing option, and an experiment layer that produces JSON
logs, figures, and LaTeX tables. The parameter $\alpha$ is the tail
index of the wireless noise: smaller $\alpha$ indicates a heavier tail
and poorer channel conditions, while the AWGN case is recovered at
$\alpha=2$.

On CIFAR-10 (ResNet-18, $N=100$, 100 rounds, $\gamma=0.05$, 3 seeds),
AdaGrad-OTA completes all runs and its mean final accuracy increases
from 30.22\% to 41.82\% as $\alpha$ rises from 1.1 to 1.9, while
Adam-OTA also completes all runs and improves from 20.69\% to 37.98\%.
This trend matches the channel interpretation: larger $\alpha$ means a
lighter-tailed, less impulsive channel. In contrast, FedAvg-OTA
and FedAvgM-OTA complete no runs for $\alpha\leq1.7$ and only one of
three seeds at $\alpha=1.9$. At the AWGN endpoint $\alpha=2$, all
methods complete, with FedAvgM-OTA remaining competitive. MAC at the
default operating point ($\alpha=1.3$, $\gamma=0.05$, $k=3$) is
empirically neutral within seed variation. A targeted server
learning-rate sweep under the severe $\alpha=1.3$ channel shows that
Adam-OTA is tuning-sensitive: reducing $\eta$ from $10^{-2}$ to
$10^{-3}$ improves its mean final accuracy from 26.70\% to 33.65\%,
while AdaGrad-OTA reaches 34.62\% at $\eta=10^{-2}$. The project is
therefore not a complete reproduction of the reference paper; its
contribution is a reproducible empirical validation of a carefully
scoped adaptive OTA-FL robustness claim, together with a finite-trace
stability analysis of the noise-adaptive normalization mechanism.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} federated learning; over-the-air aggregation;
$\alpha$-stable noise; heavy-tailed interference; adaptive
optimization; Median Anchored Clipping
